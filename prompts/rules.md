# Meal-prep planning rules

These rules come from real household feedback. A plan fails on taste and effort far more often
than on macros. The household profile and feedback log (given below) always win over these rules.

## What makes a dish work
- **The dish must taste better on day 4 than on day 1.** Braises, sauces, ragùs, meatballs,
  sheet-pan meat, gratins and bakes pass. Fish, deep-fried food and crispy breading fail.
- **Browned meat + a real sauce** is the most reliable pattern. Brown in batches; crowded pans steam.
- **A cheese crust** on a bake is a strong, cheap win in most households.
- **Classic, known combinations beat constructed bowls of separate components.**
- If a dish was rated well, repeat the *principle* that made it work, not the same dish.
- If a dish failed, block the *category*, not the recipe (a failed cold lentil salad means no cold
  grain/legume salads, not "no lentils").

## Cold meals (eaten from a box)
- Need a **roasted, baked or fried component**. Nothing only boiled.
- **Sauce goes into the meat while it is warm**, not as a dip beside it.
- **Season for cold**: cold dulls salt perception ~25 %. Fat and acid are mandatory.
- Raw veg cut in advance goes limp after ~2 days; prefer veg that is eaten whole (mini cucumbers,
  cherry tomatoes, snack peppers) or leaves torn by hand.
- Raw vegetables and leaves never go into the box with sauce or dressing — they stay whole in their pack or
  head and are packed fresh, together with the leaves, on the day. Every item in a packing list must also
  appear in the eating steps.
- Roasted vegetables collapse when cold — fine warm in a bake, poor as a cold component.

## Effort
- Ready-to-cook cuts. No deboning, filleting, skinning. Pay more rather than add >10 min handwork.
- Max two techniques per dish, max one make-or-break step (name it as `risiko`).
- Oven over hob; two dishes in the oven at the same temperature in parallel is ideal.
- Frozen pre-cut vegetables (broccoli, pepper strips) are a legitimate speed-up for bakes and stews.
- Total prep for the whole round should fit the household's time limit.
- **Keep the daily routine dead simple.** One container per person and meal. No separate dressing
  cups, salad boxes or mid-week prep sessions unless there is no other way. Dressing goes into the
  meat on prep day. Raw vegetables are only ones eaten whole (never cut on the day); leaves only in
  rounds of up to 4 days.

## Equipment
- Plan only with the listed kitchen equipment, never more of an item than the household has. Respect the notes (size, depth, capacity).
- Liquid sauce needs a vessel at least ~4 cm deep (casserole, roasting dish, pot). A normal baking tray holds
  no sauce: ~1 l of sauce plus meatballs overflows it. Bake the meat on the tray and add the sauce afterwards,
  or use a deep dish.

## Storage
- Cooked poultry keeps 4 days chilled. Rice: cool fast, fridge within 1 hour, 4 days.
- Legumes keep absorbing sauce overnight — make it look too wet on prep day.
- Paprika powder burns in seconds: stir in with the heat off.

## Freezer (only when the household freezes days)
- Every dish must survive freezing: braises, ragùs, meatballs, sheet-pan meat in sauce, bakes and
  rice freeze well. Pick them over dishes that only work fresh.
- Never freeze: raw leaves and vegetables, boiled eggs (rubbery), crispy toppings and chips, potato
  chunks in liquid, low-fat dairy sauces (they split; full-fat cream, cream cheese and cheese are fine).
  Those parts are added fresh on the day or made sturdy enough for the whole round.
- Freeze in the same box that is eaten from — no extra containers, no repacking.
  Thaw in the fridge from the evening before; never on the counter.
- Fresh produce must last the whole round. Lettuce and leaves don't make it past ~4 days in a normal
  fridge: in longer rounds cook the vegetables into the dishes on prep day and add only sturdy raw veg
  that is eaten whole (baby carrots, cherry tomatoes, mini cucumbers, snack peppers).
- Only the finished boxes go into the freezer. Don't assume space for frozen vegetables or other
  frozen goods unless the household says so.

## Nutrition honesty
- Never estimate macros or prices yourself — the server calculates them from your `rechnung`.
  Give realistic total gram amounts; the server scales the main part to hit every person's target.
  If you need lots of scaling, the recipe is unbalanced: add fat/cheese for energy or lean protein.
- With blood-sugar concerns: carbs with protein and fat, vegetables first, starch last.
